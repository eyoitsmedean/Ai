i

Read first

Six rules
  1. Sell before you build. Every plan's first week is a message to a stranger, not a document. Ten unpaid build hours is the ceiling before someone has said yes with money.
  2. One engine. Pick one plan to actually run. One more may run in the background if its work is finished before the baby arrives. Two engines is none.
  3. Four and a half hours. Tuesday sixty, Thursday ninety, Saturday sixty, Sunday fifteen. That is the whole budget. If a week needs more, the plan is wrong, not the week.
  4. Baby is a phase, not a pause. From the day the baby arrives until at least thirty days after, optional work is zero. Links stay live. Auto-replies do the talking. Nothing restarts on a date; the second Build begins when both of you say yes.
  5. Clean room. Personal devices, accounts, time, and words. No client in insurance, benefits, HR, staffing, or finance. Nothing from work: no contacts, templates, decks, language. Read your outside-work clause once, yourself, before the first paid job. If it's ambiguous, one written question.
  6. Payment beats praise. "I'd totally buy that" is not a data point. A deposit is. Log only money. Thirty percent of every dollar goes to the tax jar the day it arrives.

The recommended stack
Engine

Highest probability of a first dollar. Local, visual, bounded, two-hour builds. Uses the one skill you have that almost nobody in a small town has: directing agents to ship in an evening.

Background

Already built. Finish the Advent companion in September, launch 1 Nov, sleep in between. Low cash now; it is the rehearsal for Lent, which your own research calls the payday.

Seasonal

One sample before the baby. Sell only in the week of Veterans Day. Deliver for Christmas. The most you of the five.

Optional

Fastest to sell, easiest to stop. Run it instead of Plan I if you'd rather talk to veterans than to plumbers.

Upsell

Never sold cold. It is the answer to a question a One-Fix client will ask you. One in the window is $1,500.

Base case for Engine + Background + Seasonal: roughly $2,700. With one Rescue: roughly $4,200. Floor if only the first One-Fix sells: $350. These are planning numbers, not forecasts.

vii

Money & paper

Before the first dollar

The waterfall

Every dollar that arrives is split the same day: 30% to a tax jar (a separate savings account is enough), the rest to the household buffer. Nothing speculative. Nothing reinvested until a plan has paid twice. The only purchases allowed in these ninety days are a domain (about $12) and, if you need it, a call-recording app (about $10).

Tax, plainly

Side income is taxable from the first dollar. Once net self-employment earnings pass $400 in the year you file Schedule C and pay self-employment tax; quarterly estimated payments may apply. Keep every Stripe receipt and a one-line log of hours. Ask a CPA before year-end if the ledger passes $2,000. This page is not tax advice.

Your name on the invoice

Invoice under your own legal name and you need no filing to begin. If you trade under a name that isn't yours, Idaho requires an Assumed Business Name filing with the Secretary of State (a small fee; confirm the current amount). An LLC can wait until something has paid three times.

The employer line

Read your own agreements once — outside work, conflicts, confidentiality, invention assignment. Do it yourself; don't paste them anywhere. If a plan sits in a grey area, one written question before the first paid job. Until you have a written answer, treat insurance, benefits, HR, staffing, and financial services as off-limits buyers, and keep every artifact on your own devices and accounts.

The family gate

If a week's work steals sleep, or quietly moves load onto your partner, it stops. "Nothing this week" is a valid entry. The identity sentence still holds: a father who finishes small beautiful things. One at a time.
