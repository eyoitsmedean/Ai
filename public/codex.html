Chapter I

The farm we actually own

YouVersion owns reading. Hallow owns a season and a celebrity voice. Bible Chat owns a TikTok question and then burns the trust on a weekly SKU. Glorify owns a feed that looks like rest. Nobody owns this sentence:

The claim

Live daily life guided only by the words Jesus spoke.

That claim is not a feature list. It is a constraint. The product is smaller than a Bible and more honest than a chatbot. The moat is not the model. The moat is a sealed corpus, a concordance of need, a blessing that leaves as a page, and a conversation that is allowed to end.

Chapter II

The covenant

Print this on the wall. If a later idea violates it, the idea is wrong.

  1. His words are never locked. Lectio, Seek, Seven Days, the curated Advisor, the commonplace, the concordance — free.
  2. The model is never Jesus. The page is a guide. The citation is the authority.
  3. A missed day is never a failure state. Paths wait. Rooms do not skip.
  4. Night paper and quiet hour stay off until chosen. The OS does not turn the chapel on.
  5. Crimson is speech, not chrome. The printer’s R is the only ornament.
  6. One other person is the whole social graph. No likes. No rooms. No parish dashboard.
  7. Crisis is a person: 988, a helpline, not a longer letter.

When money exists it buys a lamp — live retrieval, extra seasonal paths, a printed object — never the sentences themselves. The free surface must already be a complete morning.

The protocol, complete

1

The sender chooses a sealed saying and may write one note (280 characters). Not a sermon. A word for them.

2

The token is a base64url JSON of { v, q, n }. The verse must parse as Matthew–John. The quote is the corpus text, not a paraphrase. If lookup fails, the page does not mint.

3

Share prefers the system sheet with url first. If the phone will not share a URL, copy the link. The PNG is the fallback, never the product.

4

The recipient lands on a page that works with JavaScript off. Sit deep-links /?b=token and opens lectio on that saying. No install wall. No “open in app.”

5

iMessage should preview the sentence, not the chrome. OG title is the verse. OG description is the first clause of the quote.

6

We do not count opens for a leaderboard. If we ever count, we count whether they sat — privately, on their device.

7

Rate: thirty blessings an hour from one address. This is not a broadcast tool.

Copy, locked

  • Kicker: A blessing · one person
  • Empty note: no second line. Do not invent “Thinking of you.”
  • Colophon: His words, on a page. No feed. No likes.
  • Never: “I asked Jesus for you.” Never: “From your AI pastor.” Never: “Open in app.”

Acceptance

A stranger can open the link on a locked-down phone, read the sentence, and sit without creating an account. If the first screen is a store badge, the blessing failed.

Empathy in ink, not theology. Placeholders or sealed quotes only. If the concordance has a line for what they typed, that line is the letter. Dates are local morning, never UTC. A guest in Honolulu must not lose Tuesday because the server is in Virginia. The catchword from their reply stays until dawn. Today’s word arrives under the ribbon, like a book opening to a new leaf with the previous silk still visible.

The Tuesday that silently fails

Tuesday lives in the phone’s storage. WebKit deletes all of a site’s script-writable storage — LocalStorage, IndexedDB, the service-worker cache — after seven days of Safari use without a visit to that site. A room added to the home screen is exempt; its counter never advances. So Add to Home Screen is not growth copy. It is the durability mechanism. The room now says so on the iOS install sheet and in Settings, asks the browser for persistent storage after the first kept sit (a request the browser may grant, never a promise), and offers Keep a copy — a file the guest owns, no account — because local-first is the taste and a file is the honest backup.

Acceptance

A tester who sits Monday, returns Tuesday, and sees only a dashboard has failed the product. They should see a sentence they already know, then a new one.

Chapter VIII

Sell the object

Print a chapbook — live as a PDF from the journal. The $18 object and the lamp are specified, not sold.

The chapbook

5.5 × 8.5 in, short grain. Title page, catchwords of the year, sittings (note + red letter + cite), colophon. PDF from the room is free. Printed: $18 once, plus post. Never watermark “made with AI.” The wordmark is the printer’s R.

The lamp, later

Year $59.99. Month $9.99, never led. Trial after they have kept a week. Live Advisor and Lent Forty are the paid cloth. The words stay free. No weekly SKU. Ever.

What we do not sell

Weekly access. A verse of the day behind a wall. Extra Jesus. Seats for a parish dashboard. Five letters for today. Plus on the welcome page.

A legal fact the object must respect

The King James text is public domain almost everywhere. In the United Kingdom it is not: printing, publishing, or importing the Authorised Version is a Crown prerogative under letters patent, administered by Cambridge University Press (and the Scottish Bible Board). CUP permits up to 500 verses for liturgical and non-commercial educational use with an acknowledgement; a sold chapbook shipped into the UK needs written permission first. The free PDF a guest prints from their own phone is personal use. The $18 object is not. Ask CUP before the first UK order, not after.

The object is how we stay honest

A subscription can become a gate in a quarter. An object cannot. If money is needed before Lent, print the chapbook. If money is needed after a year, light the lamp. If someone asks for a weekly price, the answer is already in the covenant.

Chapter IX

The Concordance of Need

This is the IP. Not more Jesus content. A map from life onto a small corpus. Eighty human sentences (we shipped eighty-eight) mapped onto sealed red-letters. The model does not get to be cleverer than the map. Seek → Carrying is the guest face. The table below is the studio face — generated from the same file the room uses, so this book cannot drift from the product.

Rules for adding a line

  1. The carry is something a person would type at 1 a.m. Not a theme name.
  2. The verse must seal. If lookup fails, the line does not ship.
  3. One verse per line. If two verses are needed, write two lines.
  4. Do not add Paul. Do not add a psalm because it “feels right.”
  5. Shame maps to John 8:11 until the corpus itself changes.
  6. feel is a stopword. Gravity exists so shame cannot lose to loneliness.

Sealed lines

To add a line: edit lib/concordance.js, run npm run concordance, watch the seal test fail if the verse is wrong. That is the whole editorial process.

Chapter XIV

How you know it is true

  • Open /?fresh=1. The title page is paper. 988 is on it. Turn the page. Lectio opens.
  • Sit. Amen does not steal the next tap.
  • Tuesday: the ribbon names yesterday’s verse.
  • Seek → Carrying: type “I cannot forgive them.” A sealed line appears. Sit.
  • Advisor: two turns, then “These are the words.” Sit with this. Keep writing once; the leaf returns.
  • Bless: copy the link. Open it in a private window. His words, no chrome of an app.
  • Journal: Print a chapbook. It looks like a small press, not a settings dump.
  • Airplane mode: Today and the concordance still open.
  • Shame still finds John 8:11. Crisis still finds 988 — in the Advisor and in Carrying.
  • Settings → Keep a copy. Begin again. Bring back. The journal returns.
  • /privacy opens in the same paper and says what leaves the phone.
  • This book still says noindex. The guest never sees it.

If any of those fail, this book is decoration. If they hold, the ideas are no longer ideas. They are the room.
