# A · One memoir scene — the case against writing this season

**Topic.** Finish one night in one room (default: Chapter 11, the apartment and the dog). Not a book plan, not twelve sittings, not a query.

**Bearing.** **C4** — One Chapter. Collides with **C3** only in November: if the household window is green, he runs Advent *or* this scene, not both, unless writing is rest.

**Why.** The 11 September archive already had the container (two nights). It under-weighted the case *against* sitting now: father-sleep, moral-injury conditions for speech, and unguided writing harm. Those three, not craft taste, decide whether the 45-minute sheet opens.

## Foundations

**Craft unit is a night, not a life.** Wolff, on the 30th-anniversary introduction to *This Boy’s Life* (LitHub, opened 14 Sep): he writes the past “in terms of stories”; “life doesn’t happen to us in stories, that we make stories out of it”; and — the line the last archive used — “It is almost always a mistake to assign a single cause to changes of heart or mind.” Alta interview with Wolff (opened 14 Sep): “You need to be as stern with yourself about your prose as you would be in a short story.” That is the sitting: one episode, stern, no invented cause.

**Voice before outline; evidence before the therapy story.** Karr, *The Writer* interview (Porter, opened 14 Sep): nine months on chapter one of *The Liars’ Club* “sorting out the distinctive voice”; “Each great memoir lives or dies based 100 percent on voice” (her book, quoted there). She had spent “20 years in therapy” before that book. She shopped for a scene that would prove her father had abandoned her; the evidence reversed it — “the view that he’d ever left me was tacit hogwash.” *The Cut* (Fischer, 22 Sep 2015, opened 14 Sep) quotes her: “Give it time — say, seven or eight years’ distance from the story you want to tell: ‘Distance frees us of our former ego’s vanities and lets us see deeper into events.’” Chapter 11 is years ago. Time-distance is not the problem. Season-distance is.

**Do not write for therapy; writing still costs the body.** Same Writer interview: chapter title in *The Art of Memoir* is “Why Not to Write a Memoir.” While drafting *The Liars’ Club* she “would suddenly fall asleep in the middle of the afternoon”; “I would sob”; she learned to take “20 minutes before your kid comes home” to “sit in a bathtub and cry.” She tells would-be memoirists to seek a therapist and “treat yourself to some kindness.” Paris Review *Art of Memoir No. 1* timed out today; the 11 September excerpt (therapy already done; the story still “standing in line”) was not re-held. NPR *Fresh Air* (Gross / Karr, page opened 14 Sep via npr.org mirror): she sends pages to living subjects; she is “never sure I’ve gotten things right.”

**Military speech that refuses a bow.** Klay, NPR *Morning Edition*, 6 Mar 2014 (text page opened 14 Sep): he wants “serious engagement with the wars… without resorting to the sort of comforting stories that allow us to tie a bow on the experience and move on.” He also says isolation is “a terrible thing to feel” if veterans cannot speak to civilians — which is a reason to finish a scene *later*, not a reason to open a hole on a short-sleep night. *Redeployment* is fiction; using “story-truth” (O’Brien) inside memoir remains a **fiction** method. Not opened as a book today.

**Healing speech is communal, not solitary.** Shay, *Achilles in Vietnam*, quoted on a bookseller excerpt page opened 14 Sep (full book not held): “healing from trauma depends upon communalization of the trauma — being able safely to tell the story to someone who is listening and who can be trusted to retell it truthfully to others in the community.” Before analyzing: “we should *listen*.” A 45-minute night alone with a laptop is not that condition. It can be craft. It is not treatment.

## Frontier

The load-bearing question this sitting asked: **should he write at all before October?** Three independent literatures say the default is no, unless the gate is green.

**Sleep is already below the adult floor.** Mellor & Van Vorst, *Workplace Health & Safety* 2015 (Sage page opened 14 Sep; N=221 fathers, 6 and 12 weeks): average aggregate sleep 5.5 h at 6 weeks, 5.9 h at 12 weeks, interrupted (2.4 then 1.8 wakes). Daytime sleepiness 40% → 64%. Fathers with DS were 12% more likely to report a workplace near-miss at 6 weeks, 14% at 12 weeks. CDC FastStats (opened 14 Sep, page dated 15 May 2024): recommended adult sleep is **at least 7 hours**; under 7 is “insufficient” / short sleep. A 45-minute night sitting is taken from the same pool as her protected block and his next-day work. Coles L. et al., *Sleep Advances* 2021 (P024 abstract opened 14 Sep): children’s sleep is associated with fathers’ sleep and with poorer general health and couple/parent–child relationship quality. The 11 September archive’s “Coles 2022” as the *<6 hour / first-12-weeks* paper was a **mis-cite**; that number is Mellor 2015. Varin et al., *BMJ Open* 2022 (abstract opened 14 Sep): at 6 months, fathers still average ~401 minutes nocturnal sleep by actigraphy and get six consecutive hours on only ~57% of nights.

**Unguided veteran writing is not a free good.** Sayer et al., *Journal of Traumatic Stress* 2015 (VA PDF opened 14 Sep; N=1,292 OEF/OIF veterans with reintegration difficulty): online expressive writing vs factual writing vs none. Small average benefits at 6 months vs no writing (ds 0.22–0.35 on PTSD, distress, anger, physical complaints, reintegration). Immediate **increase in negative emotion** on the PANAS after expressive sessions; expressive writers completed fewer sessions (2.54 vs 2.84). They **excluded** PHQ-8 ≥ 20 (severe depression “do not benefit”). Authors: not a substitute for CBT for diagnosable PTSD; “activation of negative emotion may have contributed” to dropout. No serious adverse events; 1.8–4.1% believed the study had “negative long-lasting effects” (similar across arms).

**The 10% worsening line is real — and it is not the 2015 main paper.** Sayer, Noorbaloochi, Frazier, et al., *Psychological Trauma* 2019 (PubMed abstract 30998059; full PDF not held). Latent-class analysis of the *expressive* arm (N=508): 10% “Severe Baseline Distress–Small Increase” (d = +0.23). 25% moderate distress, no change. Veterans *without* probable PTSD, with less combat exposure, and with *higher social support* were more likely to improve. Conclusion on the abstract: “Non-therapist-assisted expressive writing may be most suitable for veterans with less complex psychosocial problems.” Transfer to a literary scene is **medium**: the protocol was four 20-minute online sessions about “transition to civilian life,” not a memoir of a specific night. Direction of risk still holds: high baseline distress + no clinician is the wrong container.

**Written exposure therapy is a different object.** Sloan & Marx state-of-the-science PDF (VA, opened 14 Sep): WET is five *clinician-protocol* sessions, same event each time, first session 60 minutes. Three unguided 20-minute sessions were “insufficient” for diagnostic PTSD in their earlier work. A memoir sitting is not WET. Do not borrow WET’s safety reputation for a night in Notion.

## Objections and answers

**“The scene is years ago. Karr’s seven-year rule is already met.”** Time-distance is met (**SOURCED**, *The Cut*). Season-distance is not. Karr’s own drafting body still collapsed in the afternoon with a school-age child and a therapist. A newborn October is a worse body.

**“Sayer found writing *helps*.”** Average yes, small; they excluded severe depression; expressive sessions *raised* negative affect in the hour; 10% of the high-distress expressive class worsened (2019). A literary sitting has *less* structure than their four-session protocol.

**“Not writing is avoidance.”** Sometimes. The sheet distinguishes: gate red because of her sleep / a night that will not leave = finish. Gate green and no page for three weeks because you opened a market tab = avoidance. Mark which.

**“Wolff had to live it again; that’s the work.”** He also wrote it when he had children to leave a record for, and he *stopped* when overcome (LitHub). He did not write it in the first postpartum weeks. Obsession is a later luxury.

**“I need twelve sittings to find voice. Karr took nine months on chapter one.”** Karr’s nine months is why we retired twelve as a *program*, not why we authorize twelve now. Voice is the long book. This season’s object is one sound on one page, or a dated refusal.

# B · Verse-share / blessing as a growth act vs streaks

**Topic.** What a *first* send actually is: one person, the saying just sat with, no market. Growth that fits parked money and a newborn. Not a streak engine.

**Bearing.** **C3** — Advent door (the URL is useless if the first human gesture is a pitch). Adjacent **A2** — blessing as the growth loop.

**Why.** YouVersion’s own 2025 headline put **shares** beside installs. They also built streaks in 2017 and later had to invent a patch for the feeling of a broken chain. Habit evidence says missing one day does not kill a habit. ABS 2026 says occasional use is volatile and *plans* beat mood-scrolling for finishing the book — which is an argument for a calendar path, not for shaming a missed Tuesday, and not for making the first send a campaign.

## Foundations

**Shares are a first-party headline; they are not an A/B.** YouVersion, “Let’s celebrate what God did through you in 2025” (blog, posted 8–9 Dec 2025, opened 14 Sep): **1B+** all-time installs across the family of apps; **15.7M+** people engaging with Scripture daily; **550M+** Bible verse shares *this year*. The story they tell under the numbers is **one brother** handing the Bible App to Collin in a hospital room — not a list, not a streak. Mid-year Sowers update (search hit; full page not re-needed): 208M+ shares by June 2025. Installs ≠ people. Shares ≠ unique humans. No opened experiment says share > streak for retention.

**Streaks were a 2017 habit bet, and they count opens.** YouVersion, “Announcing Streaks” (16 Aug 2017, opened 14 Sep): streaks show “how many days in a row you’ve opened the Bible App.” Step 2 is “Connect today. (Just open the app.)” Push reminders; milestone celebrations. Requires sign-in and a network. That is a **login-and-open** streak, not a lectio streak. Perfect Weeks (May 2018) appeared in search as their own admission that a missed day feels like “disaster”; **the page was not successfully opened today** (409 / 404). Do not quote it.

**Habit science undercuts streak-break shame.** Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle, *European Journal of Social Psychology* 2010 (ISPA PDF opened 14 Sep). 96 volunteers, 82 analysable, real-world daily behaviours. Time to 95% of automaticity asymptote: **18–254 days** (median often cited as 66 from this paper’s distribution; the abstract states the range, not a single “21 days”). **“Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process.”** A missed day dropped automaticity <0.5 SRHI point; two-day rise with a miss in between was 0.55 vs 0.79 with no miss. Sample is eating/drinking/exercise, not prayer. Transfer is **medium**. Direction is enough to refuse a shame engine.

**Seasons still move people; celebrity seasons are not Dean’s object.** Hallow `hallow.com/pray40/` (opened 14 Sep): “In 2025, nearly two-million people joined Pray40, and Hallow was blessed to reach #2 in the App Store, behind only ChatGPT.” Joiners ≠ downloads. Appfigures estimates of a shrinking Lent *download pile* were opened 11 Sep, not re-opened today; keep as **ESTIMATED**, stale-by 11 Sep. C3’s decision stands: Advent week one is a dress rehearsal, money parked, organic send only.

**ABS 2026: occasional use snaps back; plans beat mood.** American Bible Society, State of the Bible 2026 ch.1 pages (sotb.americanbible.org and the 9 Apr 2026 press release, both opened 14 Sep). Fielded 8–27 Jan 2026, NORC AmeriSpeak, N=2,649. Scripture Engaged back near **17%** (2024-like). Occasional users — especially men 17%→10% — drove the 2025 spike and then dropped it. “Winging it doesn’t work”: **74%** of those who follow a plan/schedule say they have read most or all of the Bible. Movable Middle more often “read a few verses at a time and choose them based on their mood.” **INFERRED for Dean:** a calendar-opened path (*Watch with me*) is the plan-shaped object; a first blessing is not a plan. It is a gift. Do not turn the gift into a mood-scroll of 24 verses, and do not turn the plan into a streak.

**Adjacent product already says the quiet part.** Pocket Gospel (pocketgospel.app, opened 14 Sep): “I haven’t opened my bible in 6 months and I feel fake” → “Matthew 11:28 — the invitation was never about your streak.” Also: if it helps, “tell one other person who’d find it useful.” That is the growth loop that fits this household: one telling, no counter.

## Frontier

**What a first send is, if you take the sources seriously.**

YouVersion’s own conversion story is kin-to-kin, one room, no funnel. Lally says the habit does not die if Tuesday dies. ABS says the people who finish the book used a *plan*, not a feeling, and that last year’s occasional readers put the book down again. Hallow shows a season can move millions — with celebrities Dean does not have. Pocket Gospel names the shame a streak produces.

So the first send cannot be: a URL, a waitlist sentence, a second name, a “week that does not shout,” or a promise to come back tomorrow. Those are later objects (ten humans before 29 Nov; day-2 count in week one). The first send is **one named person, the saying just sat with, permission to delete**. That is growth as *gift*, which is the only growth a newborn week can keep.

No opened A/B says this outperforms a streak. The product choice is capacity design, not a growth-board win. Newborn weeks cannot protect a chain. A blessing does not require tomorrow.
