# Five things worth doing
### 5 September – 4 December 2026

The prompt in `PROMPT.md` was built from the person, not from a template, then run. This is the first output.

**Review the bound book:** open `studio/ninety/index.html`. Extreme operating detail — sittings, scripts, Advent rooms, the factory ballot — is in `PLAYBOOK.md`.

Two other agents already gave you ten income plans yesterday. This set is not those plans with new hats. It is what still makes sense after you subtract the research shelf, the newborn month, and the fact that you are already getting paid to do the thing you like.

**The real 90 days are not ninety working days.** September has maybe three optional weeks. October is a freeze you already wrote into your own constitution. November has evenings. Call it 20–35 optional hours, total. Anything that needs a funnel, a cohort, or a weekly content machine is a fantasy about a different season.

---

## 1 · The Compound
**Make Q4 at work the business that already pays the house.**

**One breath.** Treat the producer cohort and the artifacts you are already making as the 90-day project — not a side hustle you start after bedtime.

**Why you, why now.** You said it yesterday: you are being paid to spit game and write prompts, and it is awesome. Week 2 of the cohort is live this Tuesday. Enablement is being rewritten industry-wide as “the person who can make AI actually change how sellers talk.” That is the job you are already doing. A raise, a clearer seat, or a named AI-enablement lane inside a national firm is worth more than five $350 website fixes — and it uses hours you are already awake for.

**The actual work.**

- *This week:* five real opportunities, one buyer-centered first sentence each. No new slides. That is already on the work page. Do that and stop.
- *September:* one artifact per week that a seller can use without you in the room. Keep a private, clean list of *outcomes* (who used it, what changed). No employer material leaves the building.
- *October:* protect the job and the household. No optional after-hours polish.
- *November:* one page for yourself — not a manifesto — that says what you now own that you did not own in August. If a conversation about scope or title is real, have it. If it is not, the page is still the evidence.

**Done looks like.** By 4 December you can point to a cohort that ran, a handful of used artifacts, and a sentence about the seat you want in 2027. Not a side-income dashboard.

**Kill rule.** If the household gate is red, this shrinks to “keep the job safe,” which still counts. If you start building a parallel enablement product at night, you have left the compound and entered contamination. Stop.

**What it is not.** A consulting brand. A course. A LinkedIn grind. Anything that uses a client name, a deck, or a method from the day job.

## 2 · October
**Design the freeze so the other four ideas have a household to come home to.**

**One breath.** The 90-day project with the highest life ROI is getting through the month you already named: a birthday, an arrival, two young boys, a partner who does not need a third operating system.

**Why you, why now.** You have a Family page that is wiser than your Venture Studio. You have checklists for who has the boys, breakfast as a date, and “rest is legal.” You also have a pattern of opening Notion when the window is open. October will punish that pattern. A designed freeze is not quitting. It is the only way Advent, the memoir, and the job survive.

**The actual work.**

- *Before October:* name who has the boys if she goes in. Put the Tuesday work block on a calendar you will see. Pick early birthday or cake at home. That is the whole September family project.
- *October:* one weekly household window, confirmed or not needed. No optional sprint. No new agent. No “just a research hour.”
- *The only allowed night work:* something that makes tomorrow morning lighter (a bag, a meal, a text). If it is a product, it is the wrong night.

**Done looks like.** 4 December: you are still a person the household can stand. The birthday happened. The arrival had a named adult for the boys. You did not start a sixth hub.

**Kill rule.** Any no — or uncertainty — on the family gate pauses everything else. That already is your rule. Keep it.

**What it is not.** A monetizable “busy-parent OS.” A content series. A proof that you can hack family with agents.

## 3 · One Chapter
**Finish one chapter of *Our War*. Give it to one trusted reader. Stop.**

**One breath.** The book is the thing only you can make. Stop turning the veteran life into a $495 service and write the night, not the offer.

**Why you, why now.** You have a spine, a companion that is not allowed to invent firefights, and three named holes: Stockton, coming home, the apartment and the dog. Previous plans tried to productize “veteran + audio + motif.” That is a different person selling a different object. You already know the thesis: tell the story of the best people you knew; remind people they are not alone; help the people who love them understand. A finished chapter is evidence. Another capsule brief is not.

**The actual work.**

- Pick **one** hole. Coming home, or the apartment and the dog. Not both.
- Forty-five minutes, two nights a week, only in September and November. October is off unless writing is the thing that steadies you — and even then, one sitting, then stop.
- Use the companion you already wrote: stay in the room; no TED ending; never invent a name or a crash.
- When the chapter can be read aloud in under fifteen minutes, give it to one person who will tell you the truth. Not an agent. Not a publisher.

**Done looks like.** One chapter, on paper or in the existing book folder, read by one human, with three sentences of their reaction. No query letter. No website.

**Kill rule.** If a sitting turns into research about oral-history markets, you have left the book. Close the file. If the chapter starts performing for a buyer, cut it back to the night.

**What it is not.** A memoir business. Veterans Day marketing. A music capsule. Therapy. A public serial.

## 4 · Advent
**Put Red Letter in front of ten humans by 29 November. Do not add a feature.**

**One breath.** The product is built. The category’s seasonal clock is real. Your 90 days include the first week of Advent and not Lent. Ship distribution, not architecture.

**Why you, why now.** Hallow’s 2025 Advent week more than doubled downloads; their 2026 Lent week did ~750k. You will not out-celebrity that. You can own a quieter claim no one else owns: *daily life from the words he actually spoke.* You already wrote the launch doctrine: words stay free; no weekly price; never pretend the model is God. Advent 29 November is a dress rehearsal for Ash Wednesday 10 February 2027. A dress rehearsal with zero audience teaches you nothing.

Red Words (native) is a separate ship line. Do not merge the two. If you have a Mac and a Saturday in November, TestFlight can wait in line *behind* a live Red Letter URL. If you do not have a Mac, ignore the store.

**The actual work.**

- *September, one sitting:* real HTTPS URL. PWA installable. Send the link to yourself on a phone. Sit through Today once as a stranger.
- *Do not:* add Paul, a feed, a paywall, a new shell, or a second corpus.
- *November, before the 29th:* twenty-eight Advent rooms **or** reuse Seven Days four times with seasonal clothes you already designed. Name the path. Film three quiet verticals — face or handwriting, one crimson sentence, no stock doves. Send the link to ten people who will actually open it.
- *29 November – 4 December:* you are a user. Watch who comes back on day two. That number is the only metric.

**Done looks like.** A URL a stranger can open. A named Advent path. Ten sends. Three clips sitting on your camera roll (posted or not). A day-2 count, even if it is one.

**Kill rule.** If you are still redesigning the folio on 15 November, ship the current Today and stop. If day-1 lectio needs a tutorial, the onboarding is too long — cut it, do not add a help center. If you feel the urge to buy ads before ten humans have sat, you are avoiding the scare of sending.

**What it is not.** A $12 PDF business (already proposed). An App Store fantasy this quarter. A Lent campaign you try to run in September. A chatbot that talks as Jesus.

## 5 · The Factory
**Close the idea shop. Choose one existing cash lane for after October — or choose none.**

**One breath.** Your scarce resource is not ideas. It is nights, and you have been spending them on prompts that produce more nights.

**Why you, why now.** In the last thirty hours you asked agents for 90-day income plans twice, then asked again for five ideas. The studio already ranked Workflow Rescue, a paid artifact evaluator, Learning Product Rescue, High-Stakes Rehearsal, and Customer Proof. Yesterday’s folios added local site-fixes, a veteran interview, a $349 rehearsal, and “The Lamp.” Zero of these have a buyer. Another catalog is how the factory feeds. The executive project is to starve it.

**The actual work.**

- *Tonight or Sunday, twenty minutes:* read yesterday’s two folios once. Pick **one** cash lane or write `NONE` through December. Write the pick on This week. Do not build a new page for the pick.
- If the pick is **The Lamp** (paid judgment on AI L&D / GTM artifacts): freeze the M4 pack as-is. No more samples. After October, *if* the household window is green, submit one application or have one conversation. Not both. Not “to see.”
- If the pick is **a local one-fix / storefront**: do not draft a site. After October, walk into three places you already go and look at their booking path. Say the sentence or do not. Fifteen minutes each.
- If the pick is **NONE**: that is a finish. The Compound, October, One Chapter, and Advent are already a full life.

**Done looks like.** A written pick dated in September. Either one human conversation in November, or an explicit `NONE` that you do not reopen when an agent offers you a sixth menu.

**Kill rule.** The next time you type “build me 5 plans,” you are in the factory. Close the chat. If a lane needs a logo, a domain, or a Notion database, it is not a lane — it is a relapse.

**What it is not.** A productivity system. A new ranking of the old five. Permission to research “just one more asymmetric bet.”

---

## The pick

If you can only run two: **October** and **The Compound**.

They are the only two that do not require a second life. They protect the household and the paycheck that will matter when you look at a 2027 house. Everything else is optional craft.

If the household window opens in November and you still have heat: **Advent** is the only product motion that uses something you already built and a calendar date that will not move. **One Chapter** is the only meaning project that cannot be copied. Run one of those, not both, unless writing *is* rest.

**The Factory** is not a third project. It is the rule that keeps you from commissioning a fourth folio tomorrow.

---

## The factory note

Do not ask another model for five more businesses this month.

You are not under-strategized. You are over-crewed. The next useful prompt is not “what else could I build.” It is “open the URL” or “write 200 words in the room” or “five first sentences for Tuesday.”

If you want a prompt after this, use the one in `PROMPT.md` only when the calendar has changed — a green household window, a dead Advent path, a job that stopped being the thing you like. Until then, the prompt has been run.
