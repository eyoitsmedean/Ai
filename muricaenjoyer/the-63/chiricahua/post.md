>
> 70.0/100. #49 of 64. C tier.

> A standing army of 27-million-year-old volcanic hoodoos, seen by about 70,000 people a year. It slots in right behind Badlands and bumps Wind Cave to #50.

## Reply (method)

> Same formula as the other 63: scenery 7 (40%), uniqueness 7 (30%), crowd-to-wow 9 (15%), access 5 (15%) = 70.0. No editor's override. That's 48th by pure formula; Acadia keeps its published +3 bump, so Chiricahua lands #49, the middle of C tier.

## Data note

- **Status:** Chiricahua National Monument became Chiricahua National Park when the president signed the Chiricahua National Park Act on Oct 20, 2026 (Senate passage Sept 30, 2026; sponsor Rep. Juan Ciscomani). It is the 64th national park and Arizona's fourth. Sources: [DOI](https://www.doi.gov/node/68121), [AZFamily](https://www.azfamily.com/2026/10/20/chiricahuas-towering-spires-now-stand-arizonas-newest-national-park/), [KOLD](https://www.kold.com/2026/10/21/chiricahua-national-monument-officially-named-countrys-newest-national-park/?outputType=amp), [ABC15](https://www.abc15.com/news/state/chiricahua-national-part-act-signed-in-law-marking-4th-national-park-in-arizona).
- **Size and boundary:** about 12,000 acres. The act makes the park boundary the monument boundary as of enactment, so the boundary did not change; the CBO says ownership and management stay the same. Sources: [H.R. 6380 text](https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr6380/BILLS-119hr6380ih.pdf), [Senate Report 118-66](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-118srpt66/html/CRPT-118srpt66.htm). The exact NPS acreage was not retrieved, so the card says "~12,000".
- **Visits:** 70,407 recreation visits in calendar 2025, still as a national monument (2023: 62,582). The other 63 use 2024 figures; no 2024 figure for Chiricahua was retrieved, so the card shows 2025. Visits are not part of the score. Sources: [NPS Visitation Summary 2025](https://in.nau.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/212/NPS-Visitation-Summary-2025.pdf), [NPS park statistics](https://www.nps.gov/chir/learn/management/statistics.htm).
- **Signature:** the "Wonderland of Rocks", hoodoos eroded from the Rhyolite Canyon Tuff, which erupted from the Turkey Creek caldera about 27 million years ago (the NPS atlas dates the eruptions from about 26.9 million years ago; the caldera is about 12 miles across). Sources: [NPS Geodiversity Atlas](https://www.nps.gov/articles/nps-geodiversity-atlas-chiricahua-national-monument-arizona.htm), [NPS geologic formations](https://www.nps.gov/chir/learn/nature/geologicformations.htm), [Islands](https://www.islands.com/1847064/arizona-wonderland-rocks-national-monument-scenic-hiking-trails-stargazing-spots).
- **Best months:** March-May and September-November. NPS says most visitors come in March and April and July-August is quietest; the summer monsoon runs from about July through September. Sources: [NPS park statistics](https://www.nps.gov/chir/learn/management/statistics.htm), [Live That Adventure](https://livethatadventure.com/chiricahua-national-monument-how-to-spend-your-first-visit/).
- **Rank math:** 10 x (0.40x7 + 0.30x7 + 0.15x9 + 0.15x5) = 70.0. With the published tie-breakers, that is 48th of 64 by formula, between Badlands (71.5) and Wind Cave (69.0). The 12 published overrides are left as they are and Chiricahua gets none, so in the published order it goes in at #49 of 64, right below Badlands (#48), and Wind Cave moves to #50. #49 is in C tier (ranks 41-53).
- **Scores, for comparison:** Scenery 7 (Pinnacles 6, Great Basin 7, Bryce 10). Uniqueness 7 (Pinnacles 7; Bryce owns the hoodoo brand). Crowd-to-wow 9 (Great Basin 9 with 152,068 visits). Access 5 (Pinnacles 6, Guadalupe Mountains 4, Great Basin 3).
