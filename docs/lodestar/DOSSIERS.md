## 4. Offer design and the $100/hr floor
**Bearing:** C1, C3, C4  
**Why it matters.** Room $349, Storefront $595, Handoff $1,500, floor ~$100/hr after the first paid unit.

**Foundations.** Blair Enns (Win Without Pitching, written pages opened 14 Sep): four conversations; the Value Conversation sets *pricing guidance* from desired future state → metrics → value → range, **before** solutions and cost. You are free to price inputs, outputs, or outcomes. Hourly shops cluster lower revenue per person; value-priced firms jump. Jonathan Stark / “hourly is a trap” is the adjacent school — not opened as a primary book this cycle (MODEL-KNOWLEDGE). Operator Card lock: floor ~$100/hr after first paid unit or reprice / rescope / kill.

**Frontier.** Local service buyers do not sit through a four-conversation agency sale. The world-class move *for this household* is a **fixed, refundable, one-unit offer** with a kill rule — Enns’s “minimum level of engagement” in miniature. $349 / $595 / $1,500 are already off hourly. Lamp *is* hourly; that is why it is $0 until a billed hour, not a modeled midpoint.

**Live controversies.** Cheap AI-audit PDFs ($19–$99). Racing them is how Storefront becomes a $295 Saturday kit (retired). The product is a shipped Friday fix, not a report.
