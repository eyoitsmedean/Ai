# Topic Dossier — KJV licence

**Bearing.** C6  
**Why it matters.** 1,934 spoken verses on a public host.

## Foundations

US: Circular 15A rev. Apr 2026 (**VERIFIED**): works published in the US before 1931 are PD. Cornell chart current 1 Jan 2026 (**VERIFIED**): US and foreign works before 1931 PD (Twin Books exception is non-English 9th Circuit — not 1769 AV).  
UK: CUP 2021 Family Chronicle imprint (**VERIFIED** PDF): rights vested in the Crown; CUP administers under royal letters patent; liturgical/non-commercial educational use ≤500 verses and less than a full book needs no application; other uses need written permission. CoE *Brief Guide to Liturgical Copyright* 4th ed. 2024 (**VERIFIED** PDF): same 500-verse liturgical band for AV.

## Frontier

Whether a US-hosted website is a UK “publication” or “import” is **UNRESOLVED**. Official CUP HTML rights pages **failed** this session (503/403). eBible (2026-08-19) says the decree has no effect outside the UK — that is eBible’s statement, not CUP’s.

## Live controversies

1,934 > 500. A Gospels-only *spoken* extract is not a complete book, but the verse cap is already exceeded for UK liturgical no-application.

## Methods and limits

Print ` · KJV`. Do not print “reproduced by permission of Cambridge” without a grant.

## What the top 1% know

Letters patent are not a US copyright term. The wording can be PD in the US while UK print/import still needs a patentee. A speech *map* can be a new compilation (Cornell).

## Hardest objections

1. *You need CUP permission to ship Pages.* Answer: US host, PD wording; UK is a separate Dean decision.  
2. *Print the CUP acknowledgement anyway.* Answer: that claims a grant we do not have.

## Implications (what changed)
