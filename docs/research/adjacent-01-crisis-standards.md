## 1. Question

What are current (as of 2026-09-11) professional and official standards for digital tools that encounter suicidal ideation, poisoning, domestic violence, and bereavement — and what would change this product's crisis notices, false-positive policy, or disclaimers?

---

## 2. Answer

**The numbers this product already prints are still the official U.S. numbers.** 988, Poison Help `1-800-222-1222`, the National Domestic Violence Hotline `1-800-799-7233` / text `START` to `88788`, and Find A Helpline for everywhere else remain the current, defensible referrals. A reviewer who asks “are the notices current?” can be answered yes on the telephone numbers and on the order (human help before scripture; 911 / Poison Control before 988 when something has been taken).

**There is still no single global technical standard that tells a scripture advisor exactly when to fire a notice.** What exists, as of this retrieval, is a stack:

1. **Official public-health numbers and scope** (SAMHSA / 988 Lifeline, America’s Poison Centers / HRSA, The Hotline, IASP → Find A Helpline).
2. **Professional guidance for consumer AI and wellness tools** (APA Health Advisory, November 2025; IASP side event at the 79th World Health Assembly, 10 June 2026).
3. **U.S. state statutes aimed at “companion” chatbots** (New York GBL Article 47 in effect 5 November 2025; California SB 243 / Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 22601 et seq. in effect 1 January 2026). These require detect-and-refer plus “not a human” disclosure. They are written for systems that simulate a friend or partner. This product is closer to a constrained letter-writer than to that class — **INTERPRETATION**, not a legal conclusion.
4. **Website-safety practice for abuse** (The Hotline; NNEDV Safety Net): a digital-safety warning and a quick-exit control, because a trail cannot be fully erased.

**What would actually change this product, if Dean wants the notices to stay current *and* defensible under 2026 professional review:**

| Surface | Change? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 988 / findahelpline / “not a person” | **Keep.** Optional: add chat. | Numbers and disclaimer match official pages. Chat is a third official 988 channel the short notice omits. |
| 911 + Poison Control `1-800-222-1222` first when something was taken | **Keep.** Do **not** copy poisonhelp.org’s leftover `1-800-273-8255`. | Number and 911-if-collapsed rule still official. The poison site still prints the old suicide number; 988 is the current brand. |
| DV number + 911 | **Keep the numbers.** **Add a digital-safety sentence** (and, if the room is ever redesigned, a quick-exit). | The Hotline and NNEDV treat monitored devices as a first-order risk. The product currently has no such warning. |
| Bereaved vs first-person crisis | **Keep.** | Official 988 loss-survivor page supports 988 for the bereaved; it also says loss survivors are themselves at elevated suicide risk — which is why “and I want to die too” must stay a crisis. |
| False-positive policy (“a notice is cheap and a miss is not”) | **Keep as the board rule.** Do not suppress the known idioms unless Dean accepts a documented miss-risk. | Official 988 messaging is “no reason too big or too small.” Clinical NLP papers treat a missed crisis as many times worse than a false alarm. The competing cost (alert fatigue, 988 load) is real but applies more to systems that page a human reviewer than to a dismissible notice. |
| Disclaimers | **Keep.** Do not claim pastor / clinician / emergency care. Do not treat a disclaimer as the whole response. | APA requires persistent “this is AI, not a professional.” IASP’s 2026 WHA note: a disclaimer plus a list of hotlines is not a handoff. This static PWA cannot warm-handoff; clickable `tel:988` is the honest floor. |
| “Press 3” / LGBTQ 988 subnetwork | **Do not add.** | SAMHSA closed that subnetwork on 17 July 2025. Adding it would be *less* current. |
| Veterans / Spanish / Deaf paths | **Do not stuff the short notice.** Optional longer help copy. | 988’s own IVR already offers press 1 (Veterans) and press 2 (Spanish). Deaf access is videophone / 711 then 988. |

## 3. Verified numbers and scope (2026-09-11)

### 3.1 Suicide / emotional crisis — United States

**FACT** (988lifeline.org; SAMHSA 988 FAQs, retrieved 2026-09-11):

| Item | Official as of 2026-09-11 |
| --- | --- |
| Primary number | **988** (call or text) |
| Chat | **988lifeline.org** / `chat.988lifeline.org` (English); `chat.988lifeline.org/?lang=es` (Spanish) |
| Hours / cost | 24/7/365; no payment or insurance required (carrier data rates may apply to texts) |
| Scope | Suicidal crisis **and** mental-health or substance-use distress. SAMHSA: “no reason too big or too small.” Explicit examples include anxiety, loneliness, trauma, bullying, relationship trouble, “just needing someone to talk with,” and concern for a loved one. People do **not** have to be suicidal to use 988. |
| Geography | All U.S. states, territories, and tribal lands, if telephone, cellular, or internet service is available. |
| Veterans / service members / families | Call **988, press 1**; text **838255**; or Veterans Crisis Line chat. |
| Spanish | Call **988, press 2**; text **AYUDA** to 988; Spanish chat. |
| Other languages | Interpreter services on voice calls in **240+** languages (Language Line). |
| Deaf / Hard of Hearing | 988 on a **videophone** (ASL); TTY users: preferred relay or **711 then 988**. |
| Old 10-digit number | **1-800-273-8255** (1-800-273-TALK) **still works** and reaches the same network. Official brand is 988. |
| 988 vs 911 | 988 is behavioral-health support with “minimal law enforcement involvement unless there is an immediate physical safety threat.” SAMHSA lists 911 as appropriate for a suicide attempt in progress, an imminent plan with means, a suspected overdose, or physical symptoms that could be a medical emergency. |
| Geolocation | 988 does not implement precise geolocation. Georouting (approximate area, not a pin) is being rolled out on wireless; FCC deadlines for remaining carriers run into 2026–2028. |
| LGBTQ+ “press 3” / text PRIDE | **Closed 17 July 2025** (SAMHSA announcement; 988 Lifeline FAQ; The Trevor Project same-day statement). Do not print it. Trevor’s own line (1-866-488-7386 / text START to 678678) remains a separate, non-988 service — not required on this product’s short notice. |

**Product today:** “In the United States, call or text 988.” Chat is omitted. Specialized menus are omitted. That is still accurate. It is incomplete relative to SAMHSA’s three official channels.

**NNEDV Safety Net** (techsafety.org, retrieved via search + page fetch 2026-09-11) is the U.S. professional practice for *websites* that may be opened by a survivor on a monitored device: safety alert on every page, other ways to get help (voice call), and a Quick Exit to a neutral fast page. A Quick Exit does **not** delete history.

**Product today:** numbers match. No digital-safety sentence. No quick-exit. Journal and Advisor text live in `localStorage` on the device (“Wipe this device…” exists in Settings). That is a **survivorship** issue, not just a copy issue.

### 3.4 International / IASP

**FACT:**

- **findahelpline.com** (retrieved 2026-09-11): “verified helplines in 175+ countries,” maintained daily, powered by ThroughLine. The product’s “175+ countries” line matches the consumer site. ThroughLine’s own developer copy says “170+ countries” / “1,500+ services.” Use the consumer-site figure the product already uses, or say “verified helplines worldwide” if the counts drift.
- **IASP** (`iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/`, retrieved 2026-09-11): IASP is **not** a crisis centre. It partners with ThroughLine / Find A Helpline and lists “verified helplines in over 150 countries.” Older IASP SIG copy (2022) already called Find A Helpline the official IASP partner.
- **IASP suicidal-crisis page** (`iasp.info/suicidalthoughts/`): if considering suicide or in danger, call **local emergency services**; also talk to a trained helpline; see a mental-health professional; some people use a safety plan. Supporting someone: take them seriously; asking about suicide does not increase risk; help them reach a helpline.

**Product today:** findahelpline.com as the non-U.S. pointer is the IASP-endorsed directory. **Keep.**

### 3.5 Bereavement / suicide loss

**FACT** (988lifeline.org/help-yourself/loss-survivors/, retrieved 2026-09-11):

- 988 is explicitly for loss survivors (“If you’re struggling, the 988 Lifeline is always here”).
- The same page states: “Individuals who have lost a loved one to suicide are also at risk of having thoughts of suicide.” Check in; get them help if you see warning signs.
- Ongoing peer groups are a *separate* layer (AFSP’s public directory at afsp.org/find-a-support-group). 988 is not a bereavement-group directory.

**Product today:** a death by suicide or overdose without first-person crisis language opens the grief room with a 988 line and **no** Poison Control line; “and I want to die too” is a crisis. That matches official scope and the elevated-risk note. **Keep.**

## 4. Professional and official standards that apply to *digital tools*

### 4.1 There is no ISO-style “chatbot crisis algorithm”

**FACT.** IASP’s Helplines Best Practices SIG exists to share practice; it does not publish a binding detection spec. WHO LIVE LIFE (implementation guide, 2021; still the framework IASP invoked at WHA79 in June 2026) names four interventions — restrict access to means, responsible media reporting, socio-emotional skills in young people, early identification and follow-up — plus cross-cutting pillars. It is a *national* suicide-prevention guide, not an app spec.

**FACT.** The 2025 UN political declaration on NCDs and mental health (high-level meeting 25 September 2025; WHO news 16 December 2025) asks countries to prevent suicide, support people affected by suicide and self-harm, and promote responsible reporting “including online, digital and social,” and it flags digital-technology health risks. It does not prescribe notice text for a consumer advisor.

### 4.2 APA Health Advisory on GenAI chatbots and wellness apps (November 2025)

**FACT** (full text read from the advisory PDF; official APA landing page `apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps` did not return readable HTML in this environment — JS-rendered. A third-party host of the same PDF was used: infocop.es, retrieved 2026-09-11. Treat the PDF as the source of the words below.)

APA’s expert panel, for **consumer-facing** GenAI chatbots and wellness apps:

- Crisis management by these tools is “limited and unpredictable.” Relying solely on an app in an emergency is dangerous.
- Do not use them as a substitute for a qualified mental-health professional. Do not let them pose as licensed professionals.
- Developers must: disclose that the user is interacting with an AI, not a person; reduce overly human-like / sycophantic design; **integrate robust crisis-response protocols and rigorously tested escalation pathways** when suicidality, imminent harm to self or others, or other acute safety concerns are detected; and give **immediate, clear contact information for human-led services** — APA’s example is the **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline**, **clickable links**, and other validated resources.
- Policymakers should prohibit AI from posing as a licensed professional and close the gap between “wellness” marketing and actual mental-health use.

**INTERPRETATION for this product:** Red Letter is not marketed as therapy. It already does the two things APA names as the developer floor: persistent “not a person / not a clinician / not a pastor,” and a human-help notice with a clickable 988 before any scripture. APA does **not** require C-SSRS scoring or a warm transfer. It does require that the crisis path be tested — which this repo already gates (`npm run eval` crisis rows; shared `signals.js`).

### 4.3 IASP at WHA79 — 10 June 2026

**FACT** (`iasp.info/2026/06/10/online-safety-ai-and-suicide-prevention/`, retrieved 2026-09-11). Side event co-hosted by IASP, Orygen, Safe Online, and Crisis Text Line.

Shared priorities written down by IASP:

1. “a clear and adequately resourced **handoff to a trained person**, rather than a disclaimer or a list of hotlines”
2. regulation of **systems and design**, not only individual prompts
3. investment in the human services AI points people toward
4. young people as **partners** in design, not only consultees

Mark van Ommeren (WHO) tied LIVE LIFE to the digital environment. Jo Robinson (IASP President) distinguished a **validated clinical tool** from a **general-purpose chatbot** a young person opens in distress. Margaret Meagher (Crisis Text Line) described a referral pathway that is already forming — and warned that referrals often happen **without coordination, clinical standards, or resourcing** for the services that receive them. Closing line: “Human-to-human connection saves lives.”

**INTERPRETATION:** A board that only asks “did we print 988?” is asking last year’s question. The 2026 professional question is “does a person in danger actually reach a trained human, and did we dump uncoordinated volume onto 988?” This product can interrupt and hand the reader a clickable number. It cannot stay on the line, conference a counselor, or know whether 988 answered. That limit should stay visible (“this page is not emergency care”) rather than be papered over with more disclaimer paragraphs.

### 4.4 U.S. statutes aimed at companion chatbots (2025–2026)

**FACT — New York.** Governor Hochul, 10 November 2025: General Business Law Article 47 is **in effect as of 5 November 2025**. Operators of “AI companions” (systems “designed to simulate human relationships… like posing as an AI friend or romantic partner”) must (1) detect suicidal ideation or self-harm and **direct users to crisis service providers**, and (2) notify the user they are **not interacting with a human** at session start and every three hours of continued use. AG enforcement; fines fund state suicide-prevention programs.

**FACT — California.** SB 243 (Chapter 677, Statutes of 2025), signed 13 October 2025, operative **1 January 2026**, adding Bus. & Prof. Code §§ 22601 et seq. If a reasonable person would be misled to think the chatbot is human, the operator must say it is artificially generated. An operator may not let a companion chatbot engage unless it maintains a protocol that prevents producing suicidal-ideation / suicide / self-harm content, **including a notification referring the user to crisis service providers (suicide hotline or crisis text line)** when the user expresses those things. The protocol must be **published**. Annual reports to the Office of Suicide Prevention begin **1 July 2027** (counts of crisis referrals; detection/response protocols). Private right of action; statutory damages described in chaptered text as at least $1,000 per violation.

**FACT — Illinois.** Companion-safety bills in the 104th General Assembly (SB 316 engrossed text; SB 3384 introduced 4 February 2026, proposed effective 1 January 2027) would require 988 / crisis-text referral and recurring “not a human” notices. **I did not confirm enactment as of 2026-09-11.** Do not treat Illinois as binding in a ship note until Counsel does.

**INTERPRETATION — does this product fall in?** New York’s own explainer defines the target as relationship-simulating companions that remember personal details and are designed to keep users engaged. Red Letter is a constrained Gospel advisor that says, before the room opens, that it is not a person, not therapy, and not pastoral counseling. That is the opposite design. Counsel should still read the statutory definitions; this note is not a coverage opinion. The **safety floor those statutes describe** (detect expression of SI/self-harm → refer to 988 or equivalent → say you are not human) is already this product’s settled policy.

### 4.5 Research on how tools should *respond* (not inventing papers)

Retrieved, named, and dated. Where the item is a preprint, that is said.

| Work | What it actually says that matters here |
| --- | --- |
| Ngo et al., *npj Digital Medicine* (2023), “Natural language processing system for rapid detection and intervention of mental health crisis chat messages,” https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00951-3 | A production crisis-message detector. Stakeholders (clinicians, operators) set a **false-positive : false-negative cost ratio of 1:20** — a missed crisis was treated as twenty times worse than an extra review. About four in ten surfaced messages were still false positives. This is a **staffed** triage setting, not a consumer banner. |
| Szanto, Nock, et al., *Psychiatric Research and Clinical Practice* (2021), “Consensus Statement on Ethical & Safety Practices for Conducting Digital Monitoring Studies…,” https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.prcp.20200029 | Consensus for **researchers** doing real-time monitoring of people at suicide risk (contact info, weekday review, respond to imminent risk within 12 hours, automated assessment + human outreach). **Not** a consumer-app spec. Useful only to show that even research protocols put human outreach next to automation. |
| Baffsky / Robinson et al., *BMC Psychiatry* (2024), “Best practice for integrating digital interventions into clinical care for young people at risk of suicide: a Delphi study,” https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-023-05448-7 | First evidence-informed **clinician** guidelines for using digital tools with suicidal youth. Clinicians should have a general process *and* an individualised process when a young person signals risk digitally. The panel **did not** agree exact actions by risk band — clinical judgment. This product is not a treating clinician. |
| “Suicide- and crisis-risk detection using large language models in mental-health chatbots,” medRxiv 2026.01.12.26343914, retrieved 2026-09-11 — **preprint, not peer-reviewed** | On 200 labeled segments, pushing recall of high-risk cases from 98.9% to 100% lifted the false-positive rate from ~41% to ~49%. Authors argue crisis detection is inherently uncertain (clinicians disagree on the same borderline cases) and should be a **safety monitor beside** the chat model, not an accuracy contest. |
| “Developing a Tiered Machine Learning Alert System…,” medRxiv 2026.03.26.26349452 — **preprint** | In a **clinician-in-the-loop** digital-therapy product, precision was raised to cut **alert fatigue**. Tiering (no / moderate / severe) is for staff triage, not for a public scripture room. |
| OSF preprint “Development of a Consensus Statement to Guide AI Chatbot Responses to Suicide Risk Disclosure” (Delphi Feb–May 2026), https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/txpem_v1 — **preprint** | Expert + lived-experience panel, C-SSRS-styled risk bands. Validation / coping at lower risk; **facilitate crisis resources at higher risk**; encouragement to reach humans **across** levels. Chatbots were not endorsed as a substitute for crisis care. |
| Partnership on AI, “AI and Suicide Prevention: A Cross-Sector Primer,” arXiv:2605.04321 — **preprint / industry primer** | Industry is **not aligned** on when to escalate or how to do a warm handoff. PAI notes that immediately killing a conversation and pasting a number is not, clinically, the same as a warm handoff. |

**INTERPRETATION:** The research base agrees on three things and fights about a fourth.

Agreed: (1) do not let the generative model be the safety system; (2) a miss is worse than an extra notice; (3) a trained human is the destination.

Fought: how much false-positive volume is acceptable, and whether a banner-plus-number is “enough.” IASP 2026 says it is not enough *as a health-system design*. For a static PWA it is the available floor.

## 5. Competing views: over-trigger vs miss

### 5.1 The “notice is cheap” view (this product’s open question 2)

**FACT.** CLAUDE.md already records known false triggers: “hurt myself lifting boxes,” “cut myself shaving,” “overdose of caffeine,” “I don’t want to be here anymore, this party is boring,” and states “A notice is cheap and a miss is not.”

**FACT.** SAMHSA’s public 988 message is the same direction: 988 is not reserved for a confirmed suicide plan; “no reason too big or too small.” 988lifeline.org/get-help/ lists intimate-partner violence, illness, loneliness, and economic worry among reasons to call.

**FACT.** Ngo 2023’s operators chose FN cost = 20 × FP cost inside a staffed crisis queue.

**INTERPRETATION.** For *this* product the false-positive cost is not a clinician’s inbox. It is: a parchment interrupt, a short notice on a letter, and a “I am safe — continue carefully” button. The reader can dismiss it. A miss is a person who said they took the bottle and got John 14 without 911.

### 5.2 The “over-trigger harms people and systems” view

**FACT.** IASP / Crisis Text Line at WHA79 (June 2026): uncoordinated AI referrals land on human services that were not staffed for them.

**FACT.** Clinical alert-fatigue literature (the 2026 medRxiv tiered-alert preprint; general patient-safety writing) says high false-alarm rates desensitise **staff**. The same mechanism can desensitise a **user**: if the room cries crisis at a shaving cut, the next notice is easier to close.

**FACT.** 988 is a finite network (GAO-26-108114, 2026: 200+ independent centers). SAMHSA’s own FAQ says wait times vary; stay on the line.

**INTERPRETATION.** Flooding 988 with “this party is boring” texts is a *system* harm. It is a weaker argument against a **local, dismissible** notice than against an auto-dialer. It is a strong argument against adding telemetry that pages anyone, and against widening `CRISIS_RE` into ordinary melancholy (“I’m tired,” “I had a bad day”) that 988 can already take if the person chooses to call.

### 5.3 What a reviewer can defensibly be told

**RECOMMENDATION.** Keep the board rule: **prefer a false notice to a missed first-person crisis, poisoning, or violence signal.** Publish the known idioms as accepted residue, not as bugs, unless Dean explicitly accepts suppression. If suppression is wanted, suppress only the four named idioms (and any new one that tests as idiom-only in `eval/`), never “soft” signals already in the regex (`giving away my things`, `a plan and a date`). Do not replace the regex with an LLM classifier: APA and the 2026 detection preprint both treat the generative model as the thing that must **not** be the sole safety ear. This repo already moved detection into `public/data/signals.js` for that reason.

A Quick Exit (neutral news/weather page; Escape twice) is NNEDV practice, not a 988/SAMHSA requirement. It is the change that would make the *violence* surface look like the official sites a board will compare it to. That is a product decision for Dean; this note does not implement it.

**Also flag, not a notice change:** Advisor questions and journal entries persist on-device. For a person whose partner checks the phone, the notice can be perfect and the **trail** still harms them. NNEDV’s point is the trail, not the banner.

## 7. False-positive policy — explicit keep/change

**Keep:** “A notice is cheap and a miss is not” (CLAUDE.md open question 2).

**Why a board can defend it in 2026:**

1. **FACT.** Official 988 scope is broader than a confirmed plan.
2. **FACT.** Production crisis-NLP operators published a 1:20 FN-over-FP preference (Ngo 2023).
3. **FACT.** APA tells developers to fire a human-help path when crisis risk is *detected*, not when it is *clinically confirmed*.
4. **FACT.** This notice is local and dismissible. It does not dispatch police and does not open a 988 chat on the user’s behalf.

**Contrary evidence the board should hear (not bury):**

1. **FACT.** IASP/CTL 2026: uncoordinated signposting without funding the receiving service is a system failure mode.
2. **FACT.** Alert fatigue is documented in clinician-in-the-loop tools when precision is low.
3. **INTERPRETATION.** A reader who is mocked by a notice (“cut myself shaving”) may close the next one. That is the real user-side cost of the four known idioms.
4. **FACT.** 988 wait times are not zero (SAMHSA FAQ).

**RECOMMENDATION.** Do not suppress the four idioms in the name of “professionalism.” If Dean wants them gone, treat it as a product-taste decision with a written miss-risk, not as compliance with a standard — because no official standard requires idiom suppression, and several official voices run the other way.

## 8. Disclaimers — explicit keep/change

**Keep, verbatim in spirit:**

- Title-page checkbox: not a person; not therapy, medical care, or pastoral counseling; 988 / findahelpline.
- Advisor: “Not a person, not a pastor, not a clinician.”
- `CRISIS_NOTICE` / `DANGER_NOTICE` last line: “I am not a person, and this page is not emergency care.”

**Why:** APA (disclose AI; do not pose as a professional). NY Article 47 and CA SB 243 (not a human). Dean’s amendment. The product already does this *before* the room opens, not only after a regex fires.

**Do not “strengthen” the disclaimer by length.** IASP 2026 is explicit that a longer disclaimer is not the missing piece. The missing piece in the professional literature is a **handoff**. The honest sentence is already there: this page is not emergency care. The actionable line is the number.

**Small completeness items, if copy is opened:**

1. Modal and notice: “call, text, or chat 988” (SAMHSA’s three channels).
2. Danger notice: one digital-safety sentence (The Hotline / NNEDV).
3. Do not add the old 1-800-273-8255 as if it were current branding.

## 11. Contrary evidence (collected, not buried)

1. **IASP WHA79 (2026):** a disclaimer + hotline list is not a handoff. If a reviewer uses that sentence against this product, the accurate reply is: the room is a static PWA; the handoff it can offer is a clickable 988 / 911 / Poison Control / The Hotline; it should not pretend to stay on the line.
2. **Crisis Text Line / IASP:** uncoordinated referrals without funding receiving services. Mitigant: this product does not auto-connect; it signposts. Residual: every extra false notice is, in theory, one more person who *might* text 988 about a shaving cut.
3. **Alert-fatigue / precision work (2026 preprints; Ngo 2023’s 40% FP):** high sensitivity has a cost. Stronger in staffed clinical products than here.
4. **poisonhelp.org still prints 1-800-273-8255.** A reviewer comparing tabs will see a conflict. The current number is 988; the old number still works. This product should not “fix” itself by copying the stale line.
5. **The Hotline now ships an AI (“Ruth”).** That is their overflow, not a standard that scripture software should imitate.
6. **Companion statutes** may be argued either as “you are in scope” (Advisor is conversational software a distressed person might lean on) or “you are out of scope” (no simulated friend, no memory of a relationship, persistent not-a-person). Both readings exist in the open. Meet the safety floor either way.
7. **No randomized trial** shows that *this class* of notice (scripture room + 988 banner) reduces harm. APA is explicit that consumer GenAI tools lack that evidence. The standard is precaution and signposting, not proven efficacy.
